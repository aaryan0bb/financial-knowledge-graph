    Summary: This figure presents two line charts from 1988 to 2024, depicting Real Personal Consumption Expenditures (6-month annualized percentage change) and Construction Spending (year-over-year percentage change), with gray vertical bars indicating recessionary periods. Both indicators exhibit clear cyclical patterns, showing sharp declines during recessions (e.g., 1990-91, 2001, 2008-09, 2020) followed by strong rebounds. Construction spending, however, demonstrates significantly higher volatility with more pronounced peaks (up to 15%) and deeper troughs (down to -17%) compared to personal consumption, which typically fluctuates between -4% and +8%. The most recent data for 2023-2024 shows a distinct slowdown for both, with consumption growth nearing stagnation and construction spending turning negative after a post-COVID rebound.

    Explanation: The charts graphically reinforce the accompanying text's assertion of a "sharp slowdown in consumption and construction," particularly highlighting the recent stagnation in real personal consumption and a faster fall in construction spending than seen since the post-2008 housing bust. This visual evidence underscores the "Consumer remains the relative pocket of weakness" theme mentioned on the previous page, contrasting with robust corporate spending in sectors like AI and energy infrastructure.
