

GS Crypto: What (else) has changed? 

FICC and Equities | 25 July 2025 | 2:11PM UTC 

This publication is intended for clients of Goldman Sachs only. 

Bitcoin has come off lower from its recent all-time high at $123,200 and retraced to a low of 
$114,800 today (Figure 1). The cross is trading at $116,500 at the time of writing. Meanwhile 
Ethereum has continued its rapid ascent and is still hovering within reach of its YTD high of 
$3850 (Figure 1). It, however, remains >20% below its all-time-high of $4865 set in October 
2021. 

       Ethereum has moved +50% in July and +150% since April lows (Figure 1) 
       Implied volatility remains around 67 across the curve, compared to 45 for Bitcoin 
       (GS Trading) 

       Ethereum CME futures annualised premium to spot has been trading >10%, 
       overtaking that of Bitcoin (Figure 2). As a result, we have seen rotation of the basis 
       position out of Bitcoin and into Ethereum. 

       A newer phenomenon has been several publicly traded US companies 
       establishing Ethereum reserves. It is estimated that such companies bought over 
       $1.5b of ETH in the last month.

    Summary: The chart displays the 5-year history of Bitcoin (BTC) and Ethereum (ETH) annualized spot versus future funding basis, from early 2021 to mid-2025. The Y-axis represents the annualized basis in percentage, ranging from -20% to +30%. Both cryptocurrencies showed a strong positive basis (contango) in early 2021, with ETH peaking over 30% and BTC over 20%. This was followed by a sharp decline into significant negative basis (backwardation) by late 2022 and early 2023, where ETH reached a trough of approximately -20% and BTC around -15%. Subsequently, the basis for both assets recovered into positive territory throughout 2023 and 2024, stabilizing around 5-10% in 2025, though ETH generally exhibited higher volatility throughout the period.

    Explanation: The annualized basis, representing the premium or discount of crypto futures prices to their spot prices, serves as a key indicator of market sentiment and demand for leveraged exposure. A positive basis (contango) typically signals bullish sentiment and a willingness to pay for future exposure, while a negative basis (backwardation) often reflects bearish sentiment, deleveraging, or immediate spot demand over futures. The dramatic shift from strong contango in 2021 to deep backwardation in 2022-2023 and subsequent recovery mirrors the broader bull and bear market cycles of cryptocurrencies, highlighting periods of speculative fervor, market distress, and gradual recovery in investor confidence as seen in the accompanying 5-year spot price chart.
