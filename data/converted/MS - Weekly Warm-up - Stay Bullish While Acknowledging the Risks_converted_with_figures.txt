        continues to accelerate higher (now +7%), helping to confirm our 
        constructive view on the earnings backdrop post the Liberation Day 
        capitulation. An underappreciated aspect of the earnings story into 2026 is 
        that positive operating leverage is returning due in part to lower wage costs, 
        and it may be further enhanced by Al adoption. We're also not hearing a 
        significant level of concern from corporates in terms of tariff-related costs 
        impacting margins thus far, though we could hear more about this risk in 3Q. 
        With earnings on solid footing into next year and the Fed closer to cutting 
        rates, valuations can remain supported around current levels (~22x) as we 
        think about the 12-month outlook. Bottom line, we're leaning more toward 
        our bull case of 7200 for the S&P 500 by the middle of next year. 
     · Trades to Focus on ... In our view, it's too early to rotate to small caps and 
        lower quality stocks with back-end rates still elevated. Further, large cap 
        earnings revisions continue to accelerate relative to small cap revisions. 
        Thus, we maintain our preference for large caps and higher quality stocks for 
        now despite some recent froth in lower quality names. Within cyclicals, 
        Industrials continues to be our top sector pick as relative EPS revisions 
        remain strong and structural drivers should be supportive. We remain 
        underweight Consumer Goods, which is the main area of the market exposed 
        to the combination of tariff costs and weaker pricing power. Finally, we 
        reiterate our preference for US equities over international equities as 
        earnings revisions in the US materially outpace those in international 
        developed markets.
