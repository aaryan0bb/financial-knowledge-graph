 Global production forecasts drift higher 

2Q production expectations increased 190bp since March 
S&P Global estimates call for global production up 2.6% YoY in 2Q:25. This is a positive 
revision from its initial estimates in March calling for production up 0.7% YOY entering 
the quarter. Of the major regions, China production is expected to grow the most at 
 +9.0% YoY. The other major regions are expected to decline with Europe at -1.7% YOY 
and North America at -3.0% YOY. Japan/Korea is expected to be largely flat YoY.

Shifts toward light trucks continues globally 
The global shift towards light trucks continued in 2Q25. In North America, the YoY 
decline of car production was steep at -11%, which moved the mix of light trucks to 
~83% of the market. China also saw a large decline in car production at -10% YoY, 
moving the mix of light trucks to ~66% of the market. Car production in Europe 
outpaced light trucks, reducing light truck mix to ~61% of the market.

US Sales +3.0% in 2Q25 
Sales in 2Q25 increased 3.0% YOY on selling day adjusted basis. Sales were boosted by a 
surge in April (17.3mm SAAR) driven by pre-buying ahead of the announced 25% tariffs 
on non-US made autos. May and June sales slowed to the mid 15mm range after much 
of the pull forward in demand was exhausted, and OEMs began to slow incentive spend.

    Summary: This line and bar chart, "US Seasonally Adjusted Annualized Rate (SAAR)," illustrates the monthly light vehicle sales in the US from June 2001 to June 2025. The Y-axis represents SAAR in millions of units, while the X-axis spans the two-and-a-half-decade period. The blue bars show monthly SAAR, while a solid blue line depicts the 12-month moving average, smoothing out short-term volatility. The chart reveals an initial period of relatively stable sales around 16-17mm, a sharp decline during the 2008-2009 financial crisis to lows of ~9mm, followed by a strong recovery peaking around 17-18mm in 2015-2016. Post-2016, there was a gradual softening, abruptly interrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic dip in early 2020, then a fluctuating recovery. Recent data shows a notable spike in April 2025 (17.3mm) followed by a moderation to 15.3mm by June 2025, while the 12-month moving average has remained around 15.5mm.

    Explanation: The recent surge in US light vehicle sales in April 2025, which boosted 2Q25 sales by 3.0% YoY to a 16.1mm SAAR, was primarily driven by consumers pre-buying ahead of announced 25% tariffs on non-US made autos. This pull-forward of demand subsequently exhausted, leading to a slowdown in May and June 2025 sales to the mid-15mm range as OEMs also began to slow incentive spending. The long-term trend line, showing a slight downward slope, suggests a structural deceleration in US SAAR over the past two decades despite cyclical recoveries.
