                            Like many forecasters, we lowered our 2025 GDP growth forecast substantially several 
                            months ago when it became clear that tariff rates would rise much more sharply than 
                            we initially anticipated. Since then, expectations for 2025 Q4/Q4 GDP growth have 
                            remained in the 1% neighborhood (Exhibit 1, left). 

                            The activity data have now begun to show clearer support for forecasts of 
                            below-potential growth than they had at the time of prior FOMC meetings. While 
                            distortions caused by frontloading imports ahead of tariffs have made the GDP statistics 
                            hard to interpret this year, averaging Q1 GDP growth with our Q2 tracking estimate 
                            smooths these distortions and points to just 1.1% GDP growth in 2025H1. 

                            Concerningly, this deceleration has occurred before the drag from the trade war has 
                            likely peaked (Exhibit 1, right) and for somewhat different reasons than most 
                            economists expected. Consumer spending growth slowed more sharply than we 
                            expected in 2025H1 even though the great bulk of the tax-like hit to real income from 
                            tariff-driven price increases is still ahead of us. On the investment side, the weakness 
                            has come more from housing than from business investment, which should suffer at 
                            least a little from policy uncertainty and which we expect to weaken somewhat in the 
                            back half of 2025.
