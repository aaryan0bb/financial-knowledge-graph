Cyclicals Supported, Defensives Depressed 

Global fund flows, week ending July 23                                              Isabella +1(212)357-7628 Rosenberg | 
    Flows into mutual funds and related investment products showed modest flows     Goldman Sachs & Co. LLC 
    into equities and strong flows into bonds. 

    Net flows into global equity funds were soft in the week ending July 23 (+$6bn 
    vs +$5bn in the previous week). Across G10, flows were mixed; US and Japan 
    equity funds saw net outflows, while Euro area equity funds saw net inflows. 
    Investors remain focused on repatriation out of US assets; the data continue to 
    suggest that foreign demand for US equities has slowed significantly and 
    remains subdued across both Europe and Asia (see below). This is primarily true 
    in equities, while bond flows have not exhibited a similar pattern. Global and EM 
    benchmark funds both saw solid demand. Within EM, South Korea has 
    continued to see the strongest inflows in recent weeks, while Taiwan has seen 
    the largest outflows. At the sector level, industrials and financials continue to 
    attract the strongest net inflows. There has been a more notable divergence 
    between flows into cyclicals versus defensives since the beginning of July, with 
    investors showing a stronger preference for cyclicals and net selling defensives 
    (see Chart of the Week). 

    Flows into global fixed income funds were very firm, with demand benefiting 
    both government and corporate credit products (+$27bn vs +$17bn in the 
    previous week). Over the last four weeks, bank loans have seen the strongest 
    inflows as a percentage of AUM. Investors have also begun to net purchase 
    inflation-protected securities over the past two weeks. In EM, local currency 
    bond funds continue to attract very strong inflows. Money market fund assets 
    increased by $14bn. 
    Cross-border FX flows were strong. EUR continues to attract firm net foreign 
    inflows. In Asia, SGD, TWD, and HKD have seen the strongest foreign inflows in 
    recent weeks.

US" and "Euro area," from January 2024 to July 2025. Both regions began 2024 with modest positive inflows, maintaining this trend through mid-year. A significant inflection point occurred in late 2024, with Euro area flows surging to a peak of approximately +$8.1 billion in late November, while "Rest of world ex. US domiciles indicates a notable reduction in foreign investor appetite for US equity assets, consistent with trends of "repatriation out of US assets" and subdued foreign demand across major regions.

    Summary: This line chart, titled 'Flows into Cyclical vs Defensive Equity Funds (4wma)', illustrates the 4-week moving average equity fund flows in billions of USD from July 2024 to July 2025. It compares 'Cyclicals ex. Tech' (blue line) and 'Defensives' (red line). Cyclical flows exhibited high volatility, peaking at around +$2.6bn in late November 2024 before a sharp decline to a trough of -$3.4bn in early May 2025. Defensive flows generally remained around zero or slightly negative, with a brief positive period in mid-March 2025 peaking at +$0.5bn. A significant divergence emerged from May 2025, with cyclical flows rebounding strongly to nearly +$3bn by mid-July 2025, while defensive flows simultaneously dipped further into negative territory.

    Explanation: This chart, designated as the "Chart of the Week," directly supports the accompanying text's observation of a "more notable divergence between flows into cyclicals versus defensives since the beginning of July." The pronounced shift indicates a rotation in investor preference, favoring economically sensitive sectors (cyclicals) and withdrawing from more stable, defensive sectors, likely signaling increased confidence in the economic outlook.
