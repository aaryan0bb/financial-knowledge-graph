    Explanation: This chart visually supports the broader discussion on hyperscaler debt funding strategies, highlighting their historical bond issuance patterns and future projections. Despite Morgan Stanley's observation that hyperscalers could issue substantially more debt (>$500bn) without impacting ratings, the declining issuance from 2023 to 2025 suggests a potential aversion to higher on-balance-sheet debt for capital expenditures, aligning with the narrative of companies seeking alternative financing or relying more on robust cash flows for their significant capex needs, as discussed on the preceding page.

It plots two key metrics: "Equity % of S&P 500" (gold line) and "Debt % of IG Market" (blue line). The chart reveals a dramatic increase in hyperscalers' equity representation in the S&P 500, surging from around 4.5% in 2013 to reach peaks above 18% by 2024, albeit with a notable dip in late 2022. In contrast, their debt proportion within the Investment Grade (IG) market has grown far more modestly, rising from approximately 0.3% to remain consistently between 1.5% and 1.8% from 2016 onwards, showing significantly less volatility compared to their equity share.

    Explanation: The chart underscores a significant trend in hyperscaler financing: their dominant growth has been largely reflected in equity market capitalization, not through a commensurate increase in debt within the investment grade market. Despite expectations of substantial corporate debt issuance and their capacity to issue over $500bn before impacting ratings, the relatively low and stable proportion of hyperscaler debt in the IG market suggests these companies primarily fund their capital expenditures and growth through strong cash flows and equity, aligning with a stated aversion to higher on-balance-sheet debt.
